Buddy Connect is a real student program at Texas A&M, not an app I built, and for the 2023-24 year I served as VP of Operations on its leadership team. This deck is the program's own overview: who ran it, how it was structured, and what it set out to do for the students who went through it.

## What Buddy Connect is

At its core, Buddy Connect pairs incoming students with a "Buddy" — a returning student who helps them get oriented. The program deck organizes itself around three simple questions: what there is to know about Texas A&M itself, how the program actually runs, and what a student gets out of participating. It's a peer-mentorship structure rather than a technology product — the value is in the matching and the ongoing relationship, not a feature set.

## The team I was part of

The leadership team for 2023-24 was Monisha Anand as President, with the rest of us split across functions: Amarnath Saibewar as VP of IT, Vidhi Shah as VP of Marketing, Hemanth K as Treasurer, and both Radhika Patil and I as VP of Operations. Below that sat the Buddies themselves — the returning students actually doing the mentoring: Aditya Naik, Neel Gada, Udit Singh Guleria, Venitto Chettiar, Mridul Sharma, Kailash Hariharan, Paras Juneja, Prathmesh Talekar, and Dhruvi Shah. Splitting Operations across two VPs says something about the job itself — running a mentorship program at any real scale is less about a single owner and more about someone always covering the logistics: matching buddies to new students, keeping the program calendar moving, and making sure the people doing the actual mentoring have what they need to do it well.

## Closing the way the deck does

The presentation itself is intentionally light on slides — it's a team and program overview, not a pitch deck, because the real work of Buddy Connect happens in the one-on-one relationships between Buddies and the students they're paired with, not in a slide about it. It ends, fittingly, with "Gig 'em" — which is about as Aggie a sign-off as there is.

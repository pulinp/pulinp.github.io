Oculus is S.P.I.T.'s annual techno-cultural fest, and this deck is the pitch our team put together for running the 2020 edition — theme, team structure, sponsorship strategy, and the numbers behind it. It's less a single idea than a full operating plan: who runs what, how the fest pays for itself, and which new events are worth the risk.

## The theme and the team

The theme, "A Montage of Nostalgia," wasn't meant to sit on a poster — the plan was to run it through every layer of the fest: cultural and fun events built around it directly, tech events assigned specific nostalgia-adjacent domains, the physical decor and overall fest mood shaped to match, and visibility carried through the app, website, decor, and marketing consistently rather than as an afterthought. Running a fest this size meant a real org chart — separate heads for web, app, decor, graphics, cinematography, digital operations, events (split across cultural, tech, and fun), branding and social PR, operations and logistics, security, hospitality, and marketing, each with their own sub-team.

## Sponsorship and PR, beyond the obvious ask

Rather than just chasing sponsorship checks, the plan leaned on partnerships that brought their own audience. "Oculus Cafe" and "Oculus Fun Parks" proposed partnering with cafes and fun parks during slow periods to offer heavily discounted or free access under the Oculus name — cheap for the venue, branding reach for the fest, and exposure to a crowd that might never otherwise hear about it. On the talent side, brand-and-celebrity tie-ins (modeled on past examples like RadioMirchi x Sargam and Terence Lewis) let event winners get mentored or perform alongside recognizable names. PR ran on influencer invites by genre, media-house connections, a campus ambassador program, and a flash mob performed across neighboring colleges and public spots like Nariman Point in the weeks before the fest.

## New events worth trying

Alongside the returning lineup (Pro Nites, Codatron, Street Dance, SPIT MUN, hackathons, Robo Soccer, and more), the pitch proposed several new additions: an **Army Expo** bringing in the Army, Air Force (HAL), and DRDO to display equipment and technology; **Ideate**, an ideation competition built around problem statements from government bodies; **Flea**, a food-and-culture market built on partnerships with media pages and outside food brands; and industry-connect events like a virtual placement drive and internship expo run with the college's placement office and incubators. A CSR track, "Oculus for a Cause," proposed donating 10–20% of fest revenue to NGOs alongside book, clothing, and eyewear donation drives.

## The numbers and the risk

The financial plan laid out three explicit scenarios — a worst case of ₹2,70,000, an ideal case of ₹13,00,000, and a best case of ₹22,00,000 — rather than a single optimistic target. Risk management got the same treatment: emergency contacts, crowd and parking control, first-aid provisions, noise limits, and waste management were all called out as standing requirements, not contingencies to figure out later. Running an event at this scale means the fun parts — theme, talent, new events — only work if the unglamorous logistics underneath them hold up.

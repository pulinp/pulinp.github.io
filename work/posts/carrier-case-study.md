This case study looks at Carrier Corporation through two of its business lines that, on paper, don't have much in common — residential HVAC and fire & security — and asks what actually holds them together as one company. It's worth saying up front: this predates any work experience I later had with Carrier. It's a straightforward academic case study of the public business, not a reflection on that internship.

## Comfort and efficiency, tiered by budget

Carrier's residential HVAC lineup breaks into three tiers aimed at different buyers. **Comfort** is the entry point — good energy efficiency, single or two-stage compressors, standard humidity control, and straightforward user controls, built for reliability at an affordable price. **Performance** sits in the middle, adding moderate-to-high SEER ratings, enhanced humidity control, and programmable thermostats. **Infinity** is the top tier — Carrier's highest SEER ratings, precise temperature and humidity control with zoning, Wi-Fi-connected intelligent controls, and premium build quality. The case study also draws out the distinction between the two efficiency metrics buyers actually see on a spec sheet: **SEER** measures cooling output against energy consumed over a full season (typically rated 13–23), while **EER** measures the same ratio at one fixed outdoor condition, usually 95°F — SEER tells you how it performs over time, EER tells you how it performs right now.

## A different kind of value proposition: fire & security

Carrier's fire and security business is a genuinely different product category — carbon monoxide detectors and access control systems (card readers, biometric entry), plus intrusion detection through motion and door/window sensors. The fire side runs through a portfolio of specialist brands: Kidde, Edwards, Autronica, Marioff, GST, and Det-Tronics. The security side spans access control, event monitoring, intrusion devices, electronic locks, hospitality-specific solutions, mobile credentialing, real estate access, remote access management, and video management — serving residential, commercial, and industrial customers with a much wider mandate than "keep the house at 72 degrees."

## One business model underneath both

The case study ties both divisions together with a single Business Model Canvas. Key partners span raw material suppliers, distributors, cloud providers, consulting firms, and packaging companies; key activities center on providing solutions, maintenance, and R&D. The value proposition is consistent across both businesses even though the products aren't — energy efficiency, environmentally friendly operation, ease of use, reliable temperature control, and smart features, all in service of trust with the customer. Customer segments range from homemakers to businesses, factories, and office-space owners, reached through wholesale and retail channels backed by trained technicians and aftermarket support. Cost structure covers equipment, training, rent, marketing, shipping, and retrofitting of existing systems, against revenue streams built on selling equipment, providing solutions, and ongoing maintenance services. For scale, the deck cites Carrier Group sales of $20.4 billion for 2022, with 8% organic growth — a reminder that "HVAC company" understates what Carrier actually is.
